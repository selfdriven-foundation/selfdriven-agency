A place for people, organisations and communities to build and use agency. Not motivation, not hustle, not productivity theatre — real, durable agency in a society changing faster than our institutions, norms and identities can keep up with.

Why now

Skills were scarce. Roles were stable. Institutions absorbed uncertainty. Not any more.

AI, automation, remote work and institutional drift have inverted the social contract. People, organisations and communities are expected to self-navigate systems that were never designed for self-navigation.

Agency is no longer optional. It is infrastructural.

How agency builds

Agency isn't granted. It's practised.

Four moves, walked again and again — by a person, a team or a whole community. Each builds a little more internal agency, a little more external agency, or both. Each one uses a selfdriven.you mode.

  1. 01 · Curious

    Notice

    Make sense of where you actually are.

    • Person: which parts of life feel chosen, and which feel imposed?
    • Organisation: Direction, including the reflection process
    • Community: what does this place actually need?
  2. 02 · Chill

    Ground

    Pause and re-ground the “I”, or the “we”.

    • Person: name your intent and find the “no” that makes room for the “yes”
    • Organisation: Accountability, inside and out
    • Community: agree what is held in common
  3. 03 · Constructive

    Act

    Leave proof of capability, not just a claim.

    • Person: ship a rough version of the thing you keep saying you'll start
    • Organisation: Enablement and Processes
    • Community: one small, visible improvement
  4. 04 · Caring

    Connect

    Coordinate with others without coercing them.

    • Person: be the scaffold for someone else
    • Organisation: Engagement, the soul and the story
    • Community: Organisational, synchronising nodes rather than ranks

…and back to Notice. A path walked in cycles, not a ladder you climb once. Each lap starts inside and ends out in the world.

Organisations

Eight Areas of Focus.

Responsibility distributed across interconnected domains. Each area depends on and enables the others, so the organisation is both resilient and adaptive.

See the framework

  • 01Direction

    Overall direction, path, intent and design setting, including the reflection process.

  • 02Engagement

    Explainability of values, protocols and processes: the soul and the story.

  • 03Enablement

    The outputs and products of the organisation.

  • 04Protocols

    Standards, technologies such as SSI and KERI, templates and interoperability frameworks.

  • 05Sustainability

    Long-term viability, including capital for sustained human attention and conductors.

  • 06Processes

    Operational processes that drive enablement and the functioning of the organisation.

  • 07Accountability

    Internal and external accountability, including sovereign state regulatory obligations.

  • 08Organisational

    Coordination and synchronisation across the Areas of Focus, including team alignment.

Communities

The same eight, held in common.

A community has no single owner. The Areas of Focus become shared work, in service of one mission: community self-actuation.

Organising at the speed of agentic AI

  • 01Direction

    Shared intent and path, set together and revisited through reflection.

  • 02Engagement

    Why the community exists, told so that newcomers can find their way in.

  • 03Enablement

    What the community makes possible for its members and for the place it serves.

  • 04Protocols

    Open standards, SSI and KERI, so trust does not depend on a gatekeeper.

  • 05Sustainability

    Capital for sustained human attention, so stewards and conductors can keep showing up.

  • 06Processes

    Light routines that turn shared intent into action without bureaucracy.

  • 07Accountability

    To each other, and to the sovereign state's obligations where they apply.

  • 08Organisational

    Synchronisation across nodes and teams, by design rather than by hierarchy.

Ideas

Personal, but not solitary.

Choosing is one half of agency. Acting in the world is the other.

  • Internal agency

    The capacity to choose, decide and act: sense-making, understanding trade-offs, authorship over your direction and the ability to say “no”.

  • External (support) agency

    The capacity to act effectively in the world: tools, trusted frameworks, social legitimacy and the coordination that turns intent into action.

  • Who is “I”?

    When thinking is assisted and identity is spread across platforms, “I” is a locus of intent, a holder of responsibility, a signer of actions and a steward of consequences.

  • Meaningful futures

    We have an Internet of Information and an Internet of Money. The missing third helps people explore futures, test identities safely and prove capability, not just claim it.

  • The failure mode

    Systems that assume education ends, careers are linear, identity is stable and institutions know best. People are asked to self-direct without being taught how.

  • The core hypothesis

    Given the tools to understand themselves, their options and their leverage, people act responsibly without being forced.
